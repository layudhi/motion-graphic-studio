# Human character and industrial motion visual QA

## Production criteria
- Use a consistent semi-realistic 2D vector system for people, equipment, environments and overlays. Simple flat geometry is acceptable for small background figures but not for a prominent foreground safety worker.
- Check proportions, plausible neck/head junction, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, leg stance, foot contact, weight distribution, silhouette readability and frame-to-frame continuity.
- Depict required PPE visibly and accurately: hard hat, safety eyewear, high-visibility clothing with reflective stripes, appropriate boots and other task-relevant items as supported by the source.
- Hands must realistically grip radios, measuring equipment and tools. Avoid detached floating hands or intersecting tools.
- An infographic monitor may support a scene but must not replace the actual operational action. Show cause/effect and what the worker does, not only a static presentation pose.
- Do not invent site rules or imply that a single surface temperature measurement confirms the temperature throughout a stockpile.

## Story and shot options
1. Wide establishing view: worker and stockpile shown in proper scale.
2. Detail shot: handheld instrument aimed at a suitable accessible measurement point, with a restrained data overlay.
3. Follow-up shot: worker reports an observed condition using a handheld radio.
4. Closing message: prioritize a legible call to action, not a giant unrelated desktop monitor.

## Visual QA checklist
- Export full-resolution stills at entry, mid-pose and exit of each human animation, plus 360x640 phone-size equivalents.
- Review the same action in a moving clip; stills alone cannot reveal unnatural limb transitions.
- Compare before/after at matched frame positions.
- Check titles, subtitles, equipment and worker do not occlude one another.
- Record human-review sign-off; automated geometry checks only establish absence of obvious overlap.

## Gate B failure criteria
Hold approval for visibly distorted anatomy, incorrect PPE, incorrect use of equipment, incoherent action, unsafe illustrative implication, or a shot too small to convey its intended action.
